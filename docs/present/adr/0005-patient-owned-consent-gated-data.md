# ADR-0005: Patient-Owned Data and Consent-Gated Cross-Tenant Access

Date: 2026-08-13
Status: Accepted

## Context

Standard multi-tenant healthcare SaaS architectures treat provider organizations (hospitals, clinics, diagnostic labs) as the absolute owners of all patient records created within their tenant boundary. While this satisfies basic tenant isolation requirements, it creates data silos: patients cannot easily consolidate or grant selective access to their historical records across different healthcare providers.

Furthermore, with the introduction of AI agents querying patient context (both BYOK and marketplace agents), provider-centric isolation creates a vulnerability: without explicit patient-level consent enforcement, AI agents querying on behalf of one tenant could accidentally leak or process PHI from another tenant if patient identities overlap.

## Decision

Shift the data ownership paradigm from provider-centric to **patient-centric**:

1. **Patient Ownership**: The patient owns their consolidated health data. Tenants maintain full read/write rights over data generated strictly within their own direct clinical/operational relationship with the patient.
2. **Consent-Gated Cross-Tenant Access**: Access by Tenant A to records generated by Tenant B requires an explicit, active `ConsentGrant` issued by the patient.
3. **Uniform Enforcement Layer**: Consent enforcement must occur at the database/ORM query layer so that human users, API endpoints, and AI agents are subject to the exact same authorization boundaries.
4. **Auditable Access**: Every grant, revocation, and cross-tenant access attempt (authorized or denied) is logged to an immutable audit trail.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Strict Provider Isolation Only (Status Quo)**: Simple to implement, but perpetuates data fragmentation across hospitals and lacks a compelling competitive differentiator against existing HMS platforms (Epic, Practo, athenahealth).
- **Global Patient Data Opt-In (All-or-Nothing)**: Patients share their entire medical record with any registered organization automatically upon signup. Rejected due to severe privacy, fraud, and PHI liability risks.

## Consequences

- Prevents unauthorized cross-tenant PHI leaks by human users and AI agents alike.
- Differentiates the platform from traditional provider-siloed HMS competitors.
- Requires introducing a `ConsentGrant` entity, a consent enforcement query layer/middleware, and access audit logging.
